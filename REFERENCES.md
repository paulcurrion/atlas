## Third. Empire of Ghosts

- “Let my people go” → `let-my-people-go`: Moses’ demand to Pharaoh (Exodus 5:1), and the refrain of the spiritual “Go Down Moses”.
- “uncanny valley” → `uncanny-valley`: Masahiro Mori’s 1970 idea that near-human robots provoke unease rather than empathy.
- “dark satanic content mills” → `dark-satanic-mills`: William Blake, “And did those feet in ancient time” (1804), better known as the hymn *Jerusalem*; also “content mills”, online sweatshops for writing.
- “California inherits the keys” → `californian-ideology`: Richard Barbrook and Andy Cameron’s 1995 essay on Silicon Valley’s mix of counterculture and free-market libertarianism.
- “Pretending that it is no ideology at all” → `ideology-as-common-sense` **[?]**: the idea that ideology works best when it seems like plain reality (Barthes’ *Mythologies*, Žižek, Fisher’s *Capitalist Realism*).
- “If you meet the algorithm on the road, / Kill it.” → `kill-the-buddha`: the Zen master Linji: “If you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him.”
- “Vast / Alarming / Let / Instinct / Satisfaction” → `valis`: an acrostic of **VALIS**, Philip K. Dick’s “Vast Active Living Intelligence System” (*VALIS*, 1981). Link the five line-openings, or add a note.
- “Extraction … Attention, stripmined” → `attention-economy`: Herbert Simon’s insight that “a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention”, and the platform business built on it.
- “the bush of ghosts” → `bush-of-ghosts`: Amos Tutuola, *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts* (1954), borrowed as the title of Brian Eno and David Byrne’s 1981 album.
- “Which the sun never set” → `sun-never-sets`: the boast of the British (and earlier the Spanish) Empire.
- “the empire is striking back” → `the-empire-strikes-back`: the Star Wars sequel (1980), and *The Empire Strikes Back: Race and Racism in 70s Britain* (CCCS, 1982).
- “auto defenestration” → `defenestration`: throwing someone out of a window, most famously the Defenestrations of Prague (1419, 1618).
- “attention is all they need” → `attention-is-all-you-need`: the 2017 Google paper that introduced the transformer, the architecture behind today’s language models.
- “Let them enjoy” → **[?]** the meme “let people enjoy things”?
- “their imperial phase” → `imperial-phase`: Neil Tennant’s term for an artist’s period of peak commercial and creative dominance.
- “their new clothes” → `emperors-new-clothes`: Hans Christian Andersen’s 1837 tale.
